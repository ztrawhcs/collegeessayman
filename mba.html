MBA and business school essays

Business school essays are short, and there are a lot of them. Short is harder, and volume is where most applicants' voices flatten out.

Schwartz Essay Studio › MBA

The constraint is length, and it is brutal

A college applicant gets 650 words to be a person. A business school applicant frequently gets 300, sometimes twice or three times over, and is expected to cover a career arc, a reason for the degree, a post-graduation plan, and something human. Most applicants respond by compressing — cutting the specifics and keeping the summary, which is exactly backwards.

Three hundred words is enough for one scene and one idea. It is not enough for a career overview, and the résumé already handled that anyway.

"Why an MBA, why now, why here" is one argument, not three

Adcoms read the goals essay looking for a line that holds: something you are doing, something you cannot do without the degree, and a specific reason this program closes that gap. Applicants who treat the three prompts as three separate essays end up contradicting themselves across a single application.

We build the spine first, then write the essays off it. It also makes the short ones dramatically easier, because you already know what each one is for.

Older applicants have a different problem

Most of my business school clients are working adults, and the recurring failure is not weak writing — it is professional writing. Years of memos and status updates train the voice out of people. The first thing we usually do is get you to tell me the story out loud, because you are far more interesting in conversation than on the page, and then we get the page to match.
